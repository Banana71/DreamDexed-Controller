What this is for

The DreamDexed Controller is the simplest way to try out miniDexed / DreamDexed without any extra hardware – no rotary encoder, no OLED display, no DAC HAT. Everything is done from a single browser tab on your PC.

It lets you listen to the bundled Soundplantage performances right away: browse banks and programs, jump straight to a numbered performance, adjust the master volume, and forward notes from your MIDI keyboard or DAW to the Pi – all in real time, with no software to install on the Raspberry Pi itself.

This Install Guide walks you through the one-time setup: downloading DreamDexed, flashing the SD card, replacing minidexed.ini, and connecting the Pi to your PC in USB Gadget Mode. Once that is done, the Controller is all you ever need to open.

1 Before you start
What you need · the one important warning · prerequisites
USB Gadget Mode – read this once

This project is written for the Raspberry Pi 4. In USB Gadget Mode, the Pi uses its USB-C port for both data and power – the same cable that connects it to your PC.

  • Do NOT connect a separate power supply while the Pi is in Gadget Mode.
  • The Pi must be USB bus powered only – this is required by the USB specification.
  • Use the USB-C port (the one you would normally plug the power supply into).
  • Never use the blue USB-A ports for Gadget Mode – those are host ports only.
  • If you need the Pi to be powered independently (for example, to add a display later), use a USB-C Power / Data Splitter. Do not improvise this.

The MiniDexed Wiki puts it plainly: “When using a Raspberry Pi in USB Gadget mode, do not plug in its usual power supply! It should be USB 'bus powered' only.”

What you need

The bare minimum is deliberately short. A DAC, an OLED display, or a rotary encoder are not required to get started – they improve the experience later. See the Appendix if you are curious.

Qty Part Notes
● 1 Raspberry Pi 4 The smallest memory variant (1 GB) is more than enough.
● 1 USB-C to USB-A cable Connects the Pi 4 to your PC and powers it in Gadget Mode.
● 1 SD card 8 GB or larger, Class 10 recommended.
● 1 PC with browser Chrome, Edge or Brave. The Controller needs the Web MIDI API.
● 1 MIDI keyboard Any USB MIDI keyboard. Must be able to send on MIDI channel 1.
● 1 Headphones or speakers For the Pi's 3.5 mm jack. PWM output – see the Appendix for an honest word about sound quality.
○ 0–1 MIDI-to-USB adapter Only needed if your keyboard has a 5-pin DIN output.
2 The way to your first sound
Eight steps · follow them in order · about one hour
1
Download DreamDexed

You need the current DreamDexed build as a ZIP archive. Save it anywhere on your PC – you will not need it after the next step.

⬇ Download DreamDexed (build 261)

Snapshot of the continuous release. If the link ever stops working, check the DreamDexed Releases page.

The ZIP file is on your PC and has a reasonable size (~ hundreds of MB).
2
Flash the ZIP to the SD card

This turns the SD card into a bootable DreamDexed system.

  1. Install Raspberry Pi Imager or balenaEtcher on your PC.
  2. Insert the SD card into your PC.
  3. Choose the downloaded ZIP file as the source and your SD card as the target. Start the write.
The tool reports success and the SD card is ejected or ready to remove.
3
Replace minidexed.ini on the SD card

The Pi needs to know it should talk to the Controller over MIDI and use channel 10 for program changes. The quickest way: replace the file on the SD card with the one below.

⬇ Download minidexed.ini
  1. Plug the SD card back into your PC (it has a readable first partition).
  2. Find the existing minidexed.ini on the card.
  3. Replace it with the file you just downloaded – keep the exact same file name.

Prefer to keep your own file? The five lines that matter, and what they do, are in Appendix → The minidexed.ini in detail.

minidexed.ini on the SD card has today's date and contains the line USBGadget=1.
4
Insert the SD card & connect the Pi

The Pi is now ready to boot. The moment you plug it into your PC, it powers up and introduces itself as a MIDI device.

  1. Eject the SD card from your PC and insert it into the Pi.
  2. Connect the Pi's USB-C port to a USB-A port on your PC using the USB-C to USB-A cable.
  3. Do not attach any other power supply – the PC provides both power and data.
After about 15 seconds the Pi appears on your PC as a MIDI device named DreamDexed or Gadget Serial. On Windows, look in Device Manager under Sound, video and game controllers.
5
Open the Controller and connect

This is where you actually drive the Pi. Everything happens in your browser – no software to install.

  1. Open the Controller.
  2. In the MIDI OUT dropdown, select the Pi. The Controller usually detects it automatically because the name contains DreamDexed, miniDexed or Gadget.
  3. Press CONNECT.
  4. Accept the browser's MIDI permission prompt (including SysEx) – otherwise volume control will not work.
The blue OLED log inside the Controller shows “>> connected to …”.
6
Plug in headphones and set the volume

The Pi's audio output is the 3.5 mm jack on its board. Set the Controller's volume slider to something comfortable before you play your first note.

  1. Plug headphones or speakers into the Pi's 3.5 mm jack.
  2. In the Controller, set the Volume slider to about 64 (the default preset). You can refine it later.
The volume slider in the Controller reads a value between 0 and 127, and the OLED log shows a VOLUME entry.
7
Connect your MIDI keyboard

MiniDexed only makes sound when notes arrive. The keyboard can be any USB MIDI controller – it just needs to send on channel 1.

  1. Plug the keyboard into your PC and switch it on.
  2. In the Controller, open the MIDI IN dropdown and select your keyboard. Forwarding starts automatically.
  3. Set the keyboard's transmit channel to 1. This is usually a menu entry called MIDI Channel or Transmit Channel.
The green activity dot next to the MIDI IN dropdown blinks when you press a key on the keyboard.
8
Select a performance and play

The Pi comes with a curated set of performances. Each has a number between 1 and 128.

  1. On the Controller's numpad, type a number (for example 1) and press ENTER.
  2. Play a note on your keyboard. You should hear the selected performance through the Pi's headphones jack.
  3. To browse, use the PGM + / − and BANK + / − buttons, or open PERFLIST.PDF in the Controller to see which number corresponds to which sound.
You hear sound when you play. That's the whole point of this guide – you're done.
You made it

From now on, you only need the Controller – select the Pi, press CONNECT, and play.

Everything below this line is reference material. Read it only if you want to dig deeper or expand your setup.

3 If something does not work
Find your symptom below · try the first-aid trick first
First aid — try this first Unplug the Pi from your PC, wait five seconds, plug it back in. Reload the Controller page. In most cases, this alone fixes it – the Pi occasionally needs a clean re-enumeration.

I hear no sound when I play the keyboard

  • Check the MIDI IN dropdown in the Controller. It must show your keyboard, not — off —.
  • Does the green activity dot blink when you press a key? If not, the keyboard is not reaching the browser.
  • Keyboard channel: must be 1. All performances listen on channel 1.
  • Volume slider in the Controller and MasterVolume in the INI. Default is 64.
  • Headphones plugged fully into the Pi's 3.5 mm jack.

My keyboard does not show up in the MIDI IN dropdown

  • Reconnect the keyboard, then reload the Controller page.
  • On Windows: check Device Manager under Sound, video and game controllers.
  • Keyboard with 5-pin DIN output? You need a MIDI-to-USB adapter.
  • Some keyboards need a driver or a mode switch (e.g. from MIDI to USB) before they appear.

The Controller does not see the Pi

  • Use the Pi's USB-C port, not the blue USB-A ports.
  • Check USBGadget=1 in minidexed.ini.
  • Unplug and replug the USB-C cable, then reload the page.
  • On Windows: look in Device Manager for a device named DreamDexed or Gadget Serial.

Program change has no effect

  • MIDIRXProgramChange=1 in the INI.
  • PerformanceSelectChannel=10 in the INI.
  • In the Controller, the Prog Ch selector must be 10.

The PGM and BANK buttons do nothing

  • MIDIButtonCh=10 in the INI.
  • MIDIButtonNotes=1 in the INI.
  • The button mappings (MIDIButtonPgmUp=6, MIDIButtonPgmDown=7, MIDIButtonBankUp=8, MIDIButtonBankDown=9) must be present.

PERFLIST.PDF shows nothing

  • The PDF is loaded from an online mirror. Check your internet connection.
  • Reload the Controller page.
4 Appendix
Optional reading · only if you want to know more
The minidexed.ini in detail

Five lines are essential for the Controller to work. If you replaced the whole file with the one from step 3, they are already correct. If you prefer to edit your own file, here is what matters: 

# USB Gadget Mode: 1 = device mode (Pi appears as a MIDI interface)
USBGadget=1

# Enable Program Change reception
MIDIRXProgramChange=1

# Program Changes on channel 10 select performances
PerformanceSelectChannel=10

# Navigation buttons as Note messages on channel 10
MIDIButtonCh=10
MIDIButtonNotes=1
What each line does
  • USBGadget=1 — Enables USB Gadget mode. Without this, your PC cannot see the Pi as a MIDI device.
  • MIDIRXProgramChange=1 — Tells MiniDexed to respond to Program Change messages. If set to 0, program numbers from the Controller are ignored.
  • PerformanceSelectChannel=10 — Program Changes on channel 10 select performances rather than individual voices. This is the channel the Controller sends on by default.
  • MIDIButtonCh=10 — Channel on which the Pi listens for navigation button messages.
  • MIDIButtonNotes=1 — Interpret the button numbers as MIDI Note numbers (Note-On/Note-Off) rather than Control Change numbers.
Navigation button mapping (default in the file)
MIDIButtonPgmUp=6         MIDIButtonActionPgmUp=click
MIDIButtonPgmDown=7       MIDIButtonActionPgmDown=click
MIDIButtonBankUp=8        MIDIButtonActionBankUp=click
MIDIButtonBankDown=9      MIDIButtonActionBankDown=click

Full reference: MiniDexed Wiki → MIDI Button Navigation

MIDI details — channels, forwarding, SysEx
Channel assignments

The Controller sends everything it generates on a single channel – the one you select in Prog Ch. The default is 10, matching PerformanceSelectChannel=10 in the INI.

Notes forwarded from your keyboard arrive on their channel – usually 1. Keeping notes on channel 1 and program changes on channel 10 avoids “ghost notes” – a note being misinterpreted as a program change on the same channel.

Forwarding is 1:1

Every incoming MIDI message is passed to the selected MIDI OUT exactly as it arrives, on its original channel. Nothing is rewritten, nothing is filtered – with one exception: Active Sensing (0xFE) is discarded. Many older keyboards send it every ~200 ms as a keep-alive; the Pi does not need it.

Master Volume SysEx

Volume is sent as a Universal SysEx Master Volume message: 

F0 7F 7F 04 01 00 vv F7

The value sits in the MSB (not the LSB). Accepting the browser's MIDI permission prompt including SysEx is therefore required for volume control to work.

Sound quality — an honest word

Without an external DAC, the Pi uses its built-in PWM output to drive the 3.5 mm jack. On a Raspberry Pi 4 this is a simple digital-to-analog converter built from a filtered square wave – and it sounds like it.

  • You will hear a constant hissing background.
  • Depending on the Pi and the load, there may be a quiet high-frequency whine.
  • Dynamic range and stereo separation are modest.
  • It is fine for learning the interface and hearing which performance does what – but not for serious listening or recording.

The good news: a small PCM5102A DAC HAT costs under €10, plugs onto the Pi's GPIO header, and gives you a real I²S DAC with clean output. The difference is not subtle – it is the difference between “a toy that makes FM sounds” and “a proper synthesizer”.

Next upgrades and how to go further

Once the basic setup is running, there are several ways to take it further. None are required – they simply add polish.

  • Add a DAC. A PCM5102A HAT on the GPIO header transforms the sound quality. Under €10.
  • Add an OLED display. A small SSD1306 (128×64) shows the current performance directly on the Pi. Configured via SSD1306LCDI2CAddress in the INI.
  • Add a rotary encoder. A KY-040 lets you step through performances without the PC. Configured via EncoderEnabled, EncoderPinClock and EncoderPinData.
  • Create your own performances. DreamDexed uses the standard DX7 SysEx format. Any DX7 patch editor can craft new sounds.
